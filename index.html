Finding Joy in Christ

A collection of thoughts, scriptures, stories, music, and more -- centered around finding joy in Christ

By Jared Seffker

Years ago before we were all born, God made a plan and told us all about it. His Son, Jesus Christ, was at the center of the plan. Christ would create the world and we'd live there. He would come to earth one day and live His life, doing a lot of good things for people, and then sacrifice Himself for all of God's children, each of us, feeling extreme pain and then death. He would come back to life on the third day after His death, proving that He had overcome death for both Himself and all of us. Years later, that's what He did. One day, He would come back to the earth and bring everyone that followed Him to live with Him forever.

King Benjamin said "Believe in God; believe that he is, and that he created all things, both in heaven and in earth; believe that he has all wisdom, and all power, both in heaven and in earth; believe that man doth not comprehend all the things which the Lord can comprehend." There are two important invitations in this scripture. The first is to believe in God and His power. The second one can be easy to forget: believe that man doesn't understand everything God does. Sometimes when we experience trial, if we are delivered, we still feel some sadness and are disheartened, because after we thank God for deliverance, the natural man in us asks God to never let us have trials like that again. We usually won't get that answer though, because that might not be the best path for our happiness that only God understands. Sometimes after our trials we say, like kids do, "now I'm a big kid, right?" hoping that the fear and unsurety is gone for good. There's not always a great answer for why we have to endure trials, except that "man doth not comprehend all the things which the Lord can comprehend." Isaiah said, speaking of Jesus and His Father, "Yet it pleased the Lord to bruise Him." At face value, this scripture does not appear to be joyful. Yet, God knows things we don't, for our ultimate happiness. Did Christ have to endure this unsurety? Yes. He asked God to take away the bitter cup of the atoning work, but that He'd do it if it were God's will. Ultimately, God did ask Him to do it still. This shows us that even logically understanding that things are for our good is ultimately still not enough to ease the pain. Sometimes we will still have pain and there's no way around it except to just endure it, knowing that God knows best and that "man doth not comprehend all the things which the Lord can comprehend."

It's easy to imagine that Jesus would having a quality of life just was as perfect as He lived. However, Isaiah describes Him as being someone who "we esteemed him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted." Jesus lived by the same invitation He gave others, "Be not afraid, only believe." He was "a man of sorrows, and aquainted with grief." He knows the pain we experience, because He experienced it Himself. His life was hard, not only in the garden while performing His atoning work, but also throughout His whole life. Yet, He still did everything He asks us to do. He loved God and others, made time for them even while enduring His own pain and trials, was "of good cheer", gave gratitude, showed mercy, and chose faith over fear. He understands how hard life is. He knew that doing these things brings real happiness, even in pain, and so He set an example for us, so that we can try doing them as well and find happiness in Him. How did Christ accomplish all these? What kind of attitude does it take for us to try to live like He did? There's a lot of different attitudes in the world that are each intended to be a strategy for accomplishing a certain task or lifestyle. Christ's attitude was simple, but maybe surprising: "...I am meek and lowly in heart." He also said that whoever humbles themselves as a "little child", "the same is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven." Christ demanded nothing, and in the end, was given power over all things. He invites us to trust in God like He did.

"...when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously..."

The Israelites never had enough food for their journey but they always had enough food for the current day. It seems like that's how God asks us to live too. He did command them to be diligent in collecting manna each day, and He asks us to be diligent too, but if you are feeling overwhelmed, consider if you're trying to collect tomorrow's bread. If so, try refocusing on Christ and doing the other things He wants you to prioritize.

Waiting: A lot of life has to do with waiting. In life we wait for things like waiting to wake up, for mom to come home, to grow up and undestand things better, for school to start or end, for winter break or Christmas, to have more priviledges and be able to stay up later, a call or message from a friend, an invite to a party, an answer to prayer or testimony of Christ's gospel and church, good news or to "hear the bad news", the "one", to find out if they're the "one", to get into the hospital, to get out of it, healing, forgiveness, life, death, judgement, living with God again. Alma said that time only is measured unto men. So, what's the solution to all this waiting? Will we ever reach the end of it? Maybe the solution is to find enough peace to be able to wait, through Christ. Isaiah said "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on Thee."

What if God can't actually make us happy, though He can offer it, but we have to choose it? Maybe that's what we're here on earth to learn -- how to be happy through Him. Maybe that's what makes heaven so great, is the things He's taught us here about finding happiness even when there's opposition.

Does God feel pain? He lives in a state of endless happiness, and offers that to us, but we also know God weeps. Jesus promised John that He wouldn't feel sorrow, except for the sins of the world. Maybe that's what God feels.

Maybe keeping God's commandments with the main reason of what we think it will do for us in this life is not a great strategy. Even Jesus Christ, who was the least entitled of any of us, was still surprised at some of the things that He didn't realize would be taken from Him -- specifically, feeling forsaken by the Father while performing His atoning work for us. Maybe it's better to try to do it out of love for God, and maybe that's what allowed Jesus to continue trusting in God, was His love for God and us. Is faith and love the same then? Just like Christ did, there will be times when we encounter challenges that we never expected God could even rightly give us. This can shake our faith in Him, and tempt us to stop showing our love to Him by keeping His commandments. Seek charity, the pure love of Christ. Christ kept showing His love, even when He encountered things He didn't fully understand and maybe even thought He understood but then realized He did not. That's the love we need, likely, through Christ.

Imagine the strongest kid you know (maybe in the age range of like 3-4)... How strong are they? Compared to other kids, they might be pretty strong. What about compared to an adult? Probably not that strong, no matter who the kid is. The strongest kid is the one who's parent is nearby, or who stays close to their parent. It's the same with us and God. It's not our strength that the power comes from, but God's.

If you want God to teach you how to be happy, look at the members of the older generation who work in the temple. They've had a lot of the things taken away that people would say are needed to be happy, but they are some of the happiest and most purposeful people you'll meet because they trust in Christ and center their lives around Him.

As humans, we like "news". Because it distracts us? Maybe it's because as humans that endure hard lives, we want to be part of something bigger than the small, difficult problems we face personally. It's our opportunity to serve Christ, and that is something big we can be a part of.

When you're enduring hard trials you can't afford the cheap entertainment you used to enjoy that was more self-centered, but have to pursue joy in Christ. You have to try to draw near to Him. This doesn't mean you have to work super hard. Real joy in Christ can be as simple as thanking God for flowers you see while on a walk, or the sunset, or listening to a family member and offering comforting words.

Sometimes in life it's hard to accept the happiness God offers us because we still feel pain. We don't want to believe He's in control when life is still hard. God didn't promise us an easy life, but He did say "take my yoke upon you and learn of me... and ye shall find rest to your souls."

Don't worry too much about the direction your life seems to be going except whether you're developing more love and charity toward God and His children. That's what will matter most at the end of this life. Love leads us to obey God and serve Him. True love is charity, the pure love of Christ.

Paul said that if you don't have charity, you can do nothing. When Christ appeared to the disciples after His resurrection and saw that they weren't doing the work He hoped they would be, He didn't tell them to set goals, or tell them about the consequences that could be waiting -- good or bad. He simply asked them if they loved Him. "We love Him, because He first loved us." The next time you are struggling to find motivation to do something, you could try asking whether you love God, and think of all the ways He's shown love for you. Don't do it for yourself, because frankly, there's a lot God has already given us, even if we don't do everything He wants, and there's a lot of pain we might experience if we do try to do what He wants. That's the nature of having faith in Him -- we usually don't experience the full joy until after we've chosen His way. But, we can do things in love, which, although it does help us, is ultimately selfless, because we don't really know what's in store for us, but we believe in Him. Love -- charity, the pure love of Christ, acts as a power within us that we both possess but which also "possesses" us, as opposed to enmity, which is the both power satan uses and is possessed with to rule over his followers. Choosing to be possessed of charity is not a surrender of agency but a choice of how we'll use it. God said, "There is none beside Me". Ultimately, no one can tell us whether God is right, except for God Himself, and us deciding to believe Him. But, He did give us love -- love that He both showed to us but also gives us the opportunity to try and see if it is sweet. God is love, and God's gift of love is His Son, Jesus Christ. As we follow His example, follow Christ, and seek charity, the pure love of Christ, acting in love, even when it seems hard or we're unsure, we'll have the power to do what He asks as "We love Him, becuase He first loved us."

"Let us cheerfully do all things that lie in our power; and then may we stand still, with the utmost assurance, to see the salvation of God, and for his arm to be revealed." Life can be hard, especially when there are trials that seem really hard, and that seem to be stopping us from having greater happiness or accomplishing God's will.Remember this verse and remember that God is in control.

It's interesting that the adversary wanted to use fear to get people to do the right things. But, after God refused, he now uses fear to try to get people to do the wrong things. God uses love -- charity, the pure love of Christ. God's invitation for us to love Him invites us to be agents, while using fear would make us objects.

The things we learn in Christ's restored church have less to do with learning to live an easy life and more to do with learning to endure a hard one.

God said "in this world your joy is not full, but in me your joy is full." A great way to feel greater love and joy in Christ is to do things all the things in your life intentionally. Imagine you're taking a test and don't know any of the answers and can see that you'll fail it. Then, your teacher invites you to move your desk right by theirs. Every time you don't know an answer, you ask them about it, and while they don't give you the answer right away, they work through the problem with you. You know you'll receive the answer eventually, and that you'll successfully pass the test as long as you keep your desk by theirs. Most of the anxiety you had goes away, and you understand that there's really nothing to worry about anymore, as long as you follow them. It's still not easy, because some of the things they invite you to do prompt real effort and change. But, even though it's hard, you're so grateful for what they're offering you. That situation is pretty much exactly like this life. Still, the adversary will try to convince you that you're missing out -- getting a raw end of a deal, somehow. Imagine the joy you'll feel, as well as the teacher, if you try to focus instead on the teacher themselves, what they're offering to you, and try to do everything you're doing out of love to them. Every commandment God has given us is a chance to express our love for Him -- even things like cleaning, cooking for ourselves or others, going to work, going to school, going to bed on-time, and others. These are all a chance to realize how God is teaching us the answer to our "test questions", and express love and gratitude for Him as we do it.

Faith in Christ to be healed: Maybe you haven't found healing yet from a trial. You might be worried that you don't have enough faith in Christ. There are so many stories in the New Testament and elsewhere of people being healed immediately by faith in Christ. Don't worry too hard. Faith in Christ means asking for healing and then not doubting, even if you don't receive an immediate sign. That's real faith in Christ. You may not have "faith to be healed", but maybe you've been given faith in Him enough to serve others while you wait for healing, or to continue to give thanks and express gratitude, even though you feel a lot of pain and discomfort in your trial. I think God wants to see us receive healing, and sometimes we feel frustrated when we feel like we're the only thing getting in the way. However, remember that God also said, "blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed."

It's amazing to hear stories of people who have been healed through Christ, and to read stories like these in the New Testament. Sometimes we hope to have this same healing in our own life, through Christ, but how do we get it? It doesn't seem that easy. There's got to be something more, right? The woman with an issue of blood was healed immediately, after 12 years of waiting. Same with the man born blind, who was healed immediately after enduring a lifetime blindness. I'm not sure exactly what we're supposed to learn in these scriptures about receiving healing from Christ, but one thing stands out that's interesting: all of those in the scriptures who were healed found Christ.

Even if we've never heard the story in this life, we feel deep peace in the story of Christ -- A perfect lamb, a God -- The Son of God, who came to earth and obeyed His Father in every moment, and yet endured a life filled with pain, sickness, affliction, burden, heaviness, grief, sorrow -- more than any regular person will or could. Through it all He trusted, gave thanks, and then died. The earth mourned until on the third day, He rose again. He taught that He set the example for us, that through His power we could be successful like Him, and that through Him we would experience the same joy He has at the end of this life. Something about this story levels our expectations. It teaches that life is only a gift. It's a chance to love God and others, and rejoice in the gift He gives us of eternal life at the end of this life. Isn't it worth it? Is there price too great for happiness with God forever? We know that this gift is a responsibility. If we use our agency to show that we truly do not want it and can't handle it, God will give us just that. But, He also promises that there's no special skill we need to gain it except to rely on Him. There's a lot of things we can't do, but relying on someone as if we were inadequate by ourselves is something we can do.

Faith in Christ is a big deal. When we get to the next life, there won't be a special tablet that tells us who is right and wrong and who to follow throughout eternity. There's no one to defer our choice to. We must make the choice. We must make it in a way that is not dependent on anything that makes us an object, like pain or pleasure -- at least, that's not how God will have us follow Him. He will make us choose it once and then choose it over and over, even during pain and heart ache. Even faith itself, the ability to follow God more closely, God will not give it to us unless we really ask and work for it. That's one of the big purposes of this life. God will not be guilty of choosing for us or making us into objects to serve Him forever, taking away our agency in any way.

Christ endured the most painful experiences anyone on earth can imagine, and then inherited a fullness of joy in the next life. He tells us it was all worth it. We can believe Him.

It's not enough to imagine what it would be like to find peace in Christ, anymore than it is to imagine what it's like to eat food and then expect to have our hunger go away. We have to do the things He asked us to to gain it, even though they don't always make sense. We have to actually spend time in prayer and in the scriptures, even though we don't fully understand how that nourishes our spirit, or even why we need to nourish our spirit.

What does it mean to give God our will? Obviously we can't do that, but we can choose to use our will completely for His purposes, or at least try to, and continue to get better with His help. What does it mean to use our will for Him? Is it easy? It's easy when we already want to do what God is asking us, but what about when we don't? Then, maybe giving our will to God means to do things we don't want to sometimes, when God asks us to. Why don't we want to do them? Usually it's because there's some sort of pain associated with it. We tend not to worry about feeling pain when we understand it, but sometimes we don't. So, maybe giving our will to God means to endure pain for Him, even when we don't want to or don't understand. Can we endure pain for God? It's simple on paper but difficult in practice.

Why am I writing all this? Because I know God wants me to try to help others and I feel like this could be a good way to do that. There's a lot of things I'm trying to do and avoid while writing this, but, there will still be a lot of mistakes you'll find in these words and how they were written. The target audience for this is anyone who wants to hear more about Christ, and who feels like they could use more joy or relief in this life.
