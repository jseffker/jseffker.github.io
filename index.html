Waiting on Christ


A collection of thoughts, scriptures, stories, music, and more -- centered around finding joy and comfort in Christ
By Jared Seffker



Lyrics from hymn: "Praise to the Lord, the Almighty"

There was once a man who, after seeing all the problems that existed in the world and in individual lives, determined that one was more important than them all -- finding lost sheep. He devoted His entire life -- every waking moment, toward solving this problem. Everyone He met, He asked them to help. Ultimately, He died for that cause. 2000 years later, this man still lives today. He's still looking for lost sheep, and He's still asking anyone who will listen to help. He was right -- that is the greatest problem to exist, and is the greatest cause. If we choose to, we can join Him. If you do, you might be surprised to see that one of the first sheep He saves with you is yourself.

If you make a mutual promise to commit to someone fully, and then back out on that promise, you can expect that person to leave -- for good. Sin is a big deal -- so much so that God had to prepare a perfect, loving, sinless son to live a life of service and love, but also intense hardship, more than any of us could understand, and then to suffer excruciating pain and die as a sacrifice. Not only did He suffer this harsh pain, but was prepared to be able to suffer even more than man could, except it were unto death, as we read in the scriptures. The unhuman powers He had to perform healing toward all of us was potentially also what allowed Him to suffer the unhuman pain that He did. He accepted this pain out of love for God and for us, like a humble child. We shouldn't take this gift lightly. God set strict requirements throughout history for having us remember it -- the Great Sacrifice. Currently, it's the sacrament. We should try not to let this be done as a rote action or go unappreciated. God lets us experience sacrifice in our own lives too, as a testament of Him, maybe. Our own lives become a symbol of Him as we endure a small fraction of that pain -- enough to let us consider His own sacrifice. This isn't cruel of God. What greater gift could there be than to truly understand the price that was paid for us, and hopefully to live up to that in some small way? God not only gives us the gift of the Sacrifice itself, but also learning about it and teaching about it. These are not inconsequential actions. God said to Joseph Smith that our testimonies are recorded in heaven for angels to look upon. God lets us know in Isaiah that He's done this all for His sake -- not because we are good, but because He is. That means that there's basically nothing that could disqualify you from His love. He will love you anyway, and hopes that love will not be disappointed.

"...for it is mine; I have bought it with money." Sometimes we imagine God as having signed us up for something like insurance plan for our salvation -- with Himself as the "insurance agent". This model seems to hold water, until we start having problems in our life that we aren't sure are fully covered or handled in the plan's documents. Our anxiety grows when we start to feel like our "insurance agent" is giving vague answers or even avoiding calls. We wonder if He hopes that at some point we'll just "stop calling", because our problems are too complicated for anyone to have anticipated or that could be reasonably figured out within the plan's terms. Elder Renlund said "[Jesus Christ] didn’t simply sympathize with our imperfections and lament our eternal condemnation in consequence of sin. No, He went beyond that, infinitely beyond that..." Jesus not only redeemed us from the law -- He became the law. He's bought us with money, and we're not on loan. We are His. While it's true that God said that there are things we need to do to receive His Gift, association with Jesus Christ is accompanied by a growing understanding of what He can do, as shown in the New Testament of those who lived with Him in mortality. With each miracle, their previous theories of Christ's limits of power were disproven. Yes, He was Jesus Christ -- He could predict a large catch of fish, but could He feed thousands with just two? He could raise a girl who'd recently passed, but what about the man who'd been dead for four days? He could control the elements in small basins of water, but what about an entire sea whose storm threatened their lives? One dark night, He was crucified and killed, even after performing all of these miracles. Three days later, He told them from His own mouth "... it behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day". As long as we're in life, we'll have trials. One of Christ's greatest commandments to us continues to be "be not afraid, only believe."

"And they shall also be crowned with blessings from above, yea, and with commandments not a few". It can be easy to think that humans don't like rules, but, the reality is that maybe humans love rules. We make rules for ourselves all the time, and for others, and we follow them. But, humans don't always like following rules they don't understand. Sometimes we can't understand a rule until after we've followed it -- even for a long time. This requires trust, and it requires finding people you trust who you know are smarter than you. Deep down, it seems like everyone is looking for "the good life" -- the set of rules that gives them the greatest chance for happiness, and least chance for misery. God is someone who we can trust. His "rules" -- commandments, are perfect, and we can be at peace following them, even when we don't understand them.

"they were in bondage, and none could deliver them except it were the Lord their God." - the writer of the Book of Mormon about the people of Alma.

God invites us to choose Him as our master, rather than to choose fear.

There will be times in our life when, like 7 year old Joseph Smith who endured months of intense pain and then leg surgery without anesthesia, but who felt that he'd be okay as long as his dad could hold him, our only solace will be to be held by our Father -- the Savior, Jesus Christ, as we endure harsh pain. Joseph believed that if his dad could hold him during the surgery, everything would be fine. In the end, he was right -- not just during His surgery, but throughout His entire life, as his Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ held him throughout the trials of his life, including tarring and feathering, losing children, losing friends, enduring months in freezing jail cells will poor food and unsanitary conditions, and many other pains. In the end it all worked out, through Christ.

"The Son of Man hath no place to rest His head." - Christ. Have we ever felt like this -- literally or figuratively? Christ understands, and can help us.

Years ago before we were all born, God made a plan and told us all about it. His Son, Jesus Christ, was at the center of the plan. Christ would create the world and we'd live there. He would come to earth one day and live His life, doing a lot of good things for people, and then sacrifice Himself for all of God's children, each of us, feeling extreme pain and then death. He would come back to life on the third day after His death, proving that He had overcome death for both Himself and all of us. Years later, that's what He did. One day, He would come back to the earth and bring everyone that followed Him to live with Him forever.

King Benjamin said "Believe in God; believe that he is, and that he created all things, both in heaven and in earth; believe that he has all wisdom, and all power, both in heaven and in earth; believe that man doth not comprehend all the things which the Lord can comprehend." There are two really joyful invitations in this scripture: to see God's power, but also to see our own weakness. This life can be tough, even after we find healing. Sometimes after a trial we say, like kids do, "now I'm a big kid, right?" hoping that the fear and unsurety is gone for good. There's not always a great answer for why we have to endure trials, except that "man doth not comprehend all the things which the Lord can comprehend." Isaiah said, speaking of Jesus and His Father, "Yet it pleased the Lord to bruise Him." At face value, this scripture does not appear to be joyful. Yet, God knows things we don't, for our ultimate happiness. Did Christ have to endure this unsurety? Yes. He asked God to take away the bitter cup of the atoning work, but that He'd do it if it were God's will. Ultimately, God did ask Him to do it still. This shows us that even logically understanding that things are for our good is ultimately still not enough to ease the pain. Sometimes we will still have pain and there's no way around it except to just endure it, knowing that God knows best and that "man doth not comprehend all the things which the Lord can comprehend."

It's easy to imagine that Jesus would having a quality of life just was as perfect as He was However, Isaiah describes Him as being someone who "we esteemed him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted." Jesus lived by the same invitation He gave others, "Be not afraid, only believe." He was "a man of sorrows, and aquainted with grief." He knows the pain we experience, because He experienced it Himself. His life was hard, not only in the garden while performing His atoning work, but also throughout His whole life. Yet, He still did everything He asks us to do. He loved God and others, made time for them even while enduring His own pain and trials, was "of good cheer", gave gratitude, showed mercy, and chose faith over fear. He understands how hard life is. He knew that doing these things brings real happiness, even in pain, and so He set an example for us, so that we can try doing them as well and find happiness in Him. How did Christ accomplish all these? What kind of attitude does it take for us to try to live like He did? There's a lot of different attitudes in the world that are each intended to be a strategy for accomplishing a certain task or lifestyle. Christ's attitude was simple, but maybe surprising: "...I am meek and lowly in heart." He also said that whoever humbles themselves as a "little child", "the same is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven." Christ demanded nothing, and in the end, was given power over all things. He invites us to trust in God like He did.

"...until he reigns whose right it is to reign," - the Lord speaking to us.

"...when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously..."

The Israelites never had enough food for their whole journey but they always had enough food for the current day. It seems like that's how God asks us to live too. He did command them to be diligent in collecting manna each day, and He asks us to be diligent too, but if you are feeling overwhelmed, consider if you're trying to collect tomorrow's bread. If so, try refocusing on Christ and doing the other things He wants you to prioritize.

Waiting: A lot of life has to do with waiting. In life we wait for things like waiting to wake up, for mom to come home, to grow up and undestand things better, for school to start or end, for winter break or Christmas, to have more priviledges and be able to stay up later, a call or message from a friend, an invite to a party, an answer to prayer or testimony of Christ's gospel and church, good news or to "hear the bad news", the "one", to find out if they're the "one", to get into the hospital, to get out of the hospital, healing, forgiveness, life, death, judgement, living with God again. Alma said that time only is measured unto men. So, what's the solution to all this waiting? Will we ever reach the end of it? Maybe the solution is to find peace enough to wait, through Christ. Isaiah said "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on Thee."



"...For the people of the Lord are they who wait for him; for they still wait for the coming of the Messiah." - Jacob the prophet.

"Thou art Peter", "Thou art Oliver", "Thou art an elect lady", "Thou art Joseph... thou art still chosen", "Thou art mine." God reassures us of our identity and calling.

What if God can't actually make us happy, but can only offer it? Maybe we have to also choose it, and maybe that's what we're here on earth to learn -- how to be happy through Him. Could that be part of what makes heaven so great -- the things He's taught us here about finding happiness even when there's opposition?

Does God feel pain? He lives in a state of endless happiness, and offers that to us, but we also know God weeps. Jesus promised John that He wouldn't feel sorrow, except for the sins of the world. Maybe that's what God feels.

Maybe keeping God's commandments with the main reason of what we think it will do for us in this life is not a great strategy. Even Jesus Christ, who was the least entitled of any of us, was still surprised at some of the things that He didn't realize would be taken from Him -- specifically, feeling forsaken by the Father while performing His atoning work for us. Maybe it's better to try to do it out of love for God, and maybe that's what allowed Jesus to continue trusting in God, was His love for God and us. Is faith and love the same then? Just like Christ did, there will be times when we encounter challenges that we never expected God could even rightly give us. This can shake our faith in Him, and tempt us to stop showing our love to Him by keeping His commandments. Seek charity, the pure love of Christ. Christ kept showing His love, even when He encountered things He didn't fully understand and maybe even thought He understood but then realized He did not. That's the love we need, likely, through Christ.

What's the bare minimum we have to do to be saved in Christ? -- What's the bare minimum we have to eat to stay alive? We don't often ask that question. You could probably eat a lot less than is "healthy", but, you'd likely experience fatigue and potential deformity. Worse than this, you may suffer fatal injury or sickness in this weakened state. How much do you need to eat then? We can at least know that whatever you've ate until this point in life has been enough to keep you alive, if you're still alive. But, it's hard to say that your current diet is enough for tomorrow's challenges. Along these lines, the more common question for our physical health is to ask how we can be even healthier than we currently are. Regarding spiritual health, Christ said that if your hand offends you, to dismember it. He also said that He is willing to heal us from any spiritual issue we might develop. What then is the minimum we need to do to be saved in Him? Nothing? Everything? Maybe health is not so much a condition, but a desire -- to stay close to the Healer, and to always seek greater healing from Him.

The strongest kid you'll ever meet is the one who's parent is nearby, or who stays close to their parent. It's the same with us and God. It's not our strength that the power comes from, but God's.

God has taught me about being happy as I've seen the older people that work in the temple. They've had a lot of the things taken away that people would say are needed to be happy, yet they are some of the happiest and most purposeful people you'll meet because they trust in Christ and center their lives around Him.

Humans like "news". Because it distracts us? Maybe it's because, as humans that endure hard lives, we want to be part of something bigger than the small, difficult problems we face personally. It's our opportunity to serve Christ, specifically, to gather Israel, and that is something big we can be a part of, as President Nelson said.

When you're enduring hard trials you can't afford the cheap entertainment you used to enjoy that was more self-centered, but have to pursue joy in Christ. You have to try to draw near to Him. This doesn't mean you have to work super hard. Real joy in Christ can be as simple as thanking God for flowers you see while on a walk, or the sunset, or listening to a family member and offering comforting words.

Sometimes in life it's hard to accept the happiness God offers us because we still feel pain. We don't want to believe He's in control when life is still hard. God didn't promise us an easy life, but He did say "take my yoke upon you and learn of me... and ye shall find rest to your souls."

Don't worry too much about the direction your life seems to be going except whether you're developing more love and charity toward God and His children. That's what will matter most at the end of this life. Love leads us to obey God and serve Him. True love is charity, the pure love of Christ.

Paul said that if you don't have charity, you can do nothing. When Christ appeared to the disciples after His resurrection and saw that they weren't doing the work He hoped they would be, He did not lead His words by telling them to set goals, or tell them about the consequences that could be waiting -- good or bad, though He may have eventually done these. But, the first thing He told them was to simply ask if they loved Him. "We love Him, because He first loved us." The next time you are struggling to find motivation to do something, you could try asking whether you love God, and think of all the ways He's shown love for you. Don't do it for yourself, because frankly, there's a lot God has already given us, even if we don't do everything He wants, and there's a lot of pain we might experience if we do try to do what He wants. That's the nature of having faith in Him -- we usually don't experience the full joy until after we've chosen His way. But, we can do things in love, which, although it does help us, is ultimately selfless, because we don't really know what's in store for us, but we believe in Him. Love -- charity, the pure love of Christ, acts as a power within us that we both possess but which also "possesses" us, as opposed to enmity, which is the both power satan uses and is possessed with to rule over his followers. Choosing to be possessed of charity is not a surrender of agency but a choice of how we'll use it. God said, "There is none beside Me". Ultimately, no one can tell us whether God is right, except for God Himself, and us deciding to believe Him. But, He did give us love -- love that He both showed to us but also gives us the opportunity to try and see if it is sweet. God is love, and God's gift of love is His Son, Jesus Christ. As we follow His example, follow Christ, and seek charity, the pure love of Christ, acting in love, even when it seems hard or we're unsure, we'll have the power to do what He asks as "We love Him, becuase He first loved us."

"Let us cheerfully do all things that lie in our power; and then may we stand still, with the utmost assurance, to see the salvation of God, and for his arm to be revealed." Life can be hard, especially when we have hard trials that seem to stop us from having greater happiness or accomplishing God's will. Remember this verse and remember that God is in control.

The adversary once offered to use fear to get us to do the right things. Now he tries to use it to get us to do the wrong things. God uses love -- charity, the pure love of Christ. God's invitation for us to love Him invites us to be agents, while using fear would make us objects.

The things we learn in Christ's restored church have less to do with living an easy life and more to do with enduring a hard one.

God said "in this world your joy is not full, but in me your joy is full." A great way to feel greater love and joy in Christ is to do things all the things in your life intentionally. Imagine you're taking a test and don't know any of the answers and can see that you'll fail it. Then, your teacher invites you to move your desk right by theirs. Every time you don't know an answer, you ask them about it, and while they don't give you the answer right away, they work through the problem with you. You know you'll receive the answer eventually, and that you'll successfully pass the test as long as you keep your desk by theirs. Most of the anxiety you had goes away, and you understand that there's really nothing to worry about anymore, as long as you follow them. It's still not easy, because some of the things they invite you to do prompt real effort and change. But, even though it's hard, you're so grateful for what they're offering you. That situation is pretty much exactly like this life. Still, the adversary will try to convince you that you're missing out -- getting a raw end of a deal, somehow. Imagine the joy you'll feel, as well as the teacher, if you try to focus instead on the teacher themselves, what they're offering to you, and try to do everything you're doing out of love to them. Every commandment God has given us is a chance to express our love for Him -- even things like cleaning, cooking for ourselves or others, going to work, going to school, going to bed on-time, and others. These are all a chance to realize how God is teaching us the answer to our "test questions", and express love and gratitude for Him as we do it.

Sometimes it's hard to understand what God is doing in our life. What's His purpose? We know that trial is worth it, but why is it necessary at all? We know that Christ accomplished the atonement to heal us -- He instituted the sacrament so that we can always rejoice in this gift -- everything is created to bear record of Him. But, we know that life must continue to be difficult. God said that our pain will be for our good. Remember that God is giving us a gift in all of this trial. More than just a reward in the eternities, our suffering may be our reward, in part. Think of soldiers in a boot camp. What are they really gaining? Does it really make soldiers healthier, or stronger, or more skilled to have them go through hard things that almost seem to break them? Maybe one of the most valuable things that a soldier gains in training is to not be so easily led by fear. It's hard to choose it in the moment, but it's worth it before, and after -- not just to the leadership, but the soldier themselves. We chose it too, both before this life, and we'll be glad for it after -- to have greater trust in God -- to know by experience how to put Him first, even when there are vicious pains and fears in our path. You may not feel like your life is amounting to much, but if you're gaining greater trust in God through it, and gaining real experience on following His commandments through it all, you may be surprised how useful of a "soldier" God has made you in His army one day. I imagine that one day we'll leave this life, and we'll see some of those we knew in this life, who knew and loved God -- those with physical challenges that kept them isolated; those with mental handicaps; those with severe family or social challenges; those in less fortunate circumstances of finance or opportunity; the list goes on. We might have wondered in this life why they had to go through it all. Sure, the reward would have far outweighed any hardship -- but why the hardship in the first place, and why so long? But, seeing them there, in the next life, we might be surprised that God had prepared them as His finest war generals. Their meekness might be turned to piercing honor -- their loving attitude, an unbreakable endurance and love for Christ. Maybe then we'll understand why their calling was different than others'. They might feel alienated, but they're not alone, as Christ felt this way and helps us and others through it. The Book of Mormon writer describes a warring nation, that, within it was a unique group who's situation goes: "...they did look upon shedding the blood of their brethren with the greatest abhorrence; and they never could be prevailed upon to take up arms against their brethren." The more we read about these people, the more we understand how their situation was justified and righteous. Still, we know they sometimes experienced doubt about this decision, and they may have experienced pressure from others too. But, they had received a witness of God's approval, and were committed to obeying Him. The account continues: "...thus they were a zealous and beloved people, a highly favored people of the Lord." It's hard to know exactly what they might have contributed in their unqiue situation, but, one account goes as follows: "the chief ruler of the Zoramites, being a very wicked man, sent over unto the people of Ammon desiring them that they should cast out of their land all those who came over from them into their land. And he breathed out many threatenings against them. And now the people of Ammon did not fear their words; therefore they did not cast them out, but they did receive all the poor of the Zoramites that came over unto them;" We don't know everything God gave them, but we do know that they had no fear, because of Christ. "...they never did look upon death with any degree of terror, for their hope and views of Christ". In a war that's already been won by Christ, the only thing we need then is the courage to fight.

Faith in Christ to be healed: Maybe you haven't found healing yet from a trial. You might be worried that you don't have enough faith in Christ. There are so many stories in the New Testament and elsewhere of people being healed immediately by faith in Christ. Don't worry too hard. Faith in Christ means asking for healing and then not doubting, even if you don't receive an immediate sign. That's real faith in Christ. You may not have "faith to be healed", but maybe you've been given faith in Him enough to serve others while you wait for healing, or to continue to give thanks and express gratitude, even though you feel a lot of pain and discomfort in your trial. I think God wants to see us receive healing, and sometimes we feel frustrated when we feel like we're the only thing getting in the way. However, remember that God also said, "blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed."

What really is the gift of healing? The ability to follow Christ and encourage others to do the same? Any time you encourage someone to follow Christ more obediently, or one of His appointed servants, you are using the gift of healing, which is Christ.

It's amazing to hear stories of people who have been healed through Christ, and to read stories like these in the New Testament. Sometimes we hope to have this same healing in our own life, through Christ, but how do we get it? It doesn't seem that easy. There's got to be something more, right? The woman with an issue of blood was healed immediately, after 12 years of waiting. Same with the man born blind, who was healed immediately after enduring a lifetime blindness. I'm not sure exactly what we're supposed to learn from these scriptures, but one thing stands out: all of those who were healed first found Christ.

Even if we've never heard the story in this life, we feel deep peace in the story of Christ -- A perfect lamb, a God -- The Son of God, who came to earth and obeyed His Father in every moment, and yet endured a life filled with pain, sickness, affliction, burden, heaviness, grief, sorrow -- more than any regular person will or could. Through it all He trusted, gave thanks, and then died. The earth mourned until on the third day, He rose again. He taught that He set the example for us, that through His power we could be successful like Him, and that through Him we would experience the same joy He has at the end of this life. Something about this story levels our expectations. It teaches that life is only a gift. It's a chance to love God and others, and rejoice in the gift He gives us of eternal life at the end of this life. Isn't it worth it? Is there price too great for happiness with God forever? We know that this gift is a responsibility. If we use our agency to show that we truly do not want it and can't handle it, God will give us just that. But, He also promises that there's no special skill we need to gain it except to rely on Him. There's a lot of things we can't do, but relying on someone as if we were inadequate by ourselves is something we can do.

Faith in Christ is a big deal. When we get to the next life, there won't be a special tablet that tells us who is right and wrong and who to follow throughout eternity. There's no one to defer our choice to. We must make the choice. We must make it in a way that is not dependent on anything that makes us an object, like pain or pleasure -- at least, that's not how God will have us follow Him. He will make us choose it once and then choose it over and over, even during pain and heart ache. Even faith itself, the ability to follow God more closely, God will not give it to us unless we really ask and work for it. That's one of the big purposes of this life. God will not be guilty of choosing for us or making us into objects to serve Him forever, taking away our agency in any way.

Christ endured the most painful experiences anyone on earth can imagine, and then inherited a fullness of joy in the next life. He tells us it was all worth it. We can believe Him.

It's not enough to imagine what it would be like to find peace in Christ, anymore than it is to imagine what it's like to eat food rather than actually eating it. We have to do the things He asked us to to gain it, even though they don't always make sense. We have to actually spend time in prayer and in the scriptures, even though we don't fully understand how that nourishes our spirit, or even why we need to nourish our spirit.

What does it mean to give God our will? Obviously we can't do that, but we can choose to use our will completely for His purposes, or at least try to, and continue to get better with His help. What does it mean to use our will for Him? Is it easy? It's easy when we already want to do what God is asking us, but what about when we don't? Then, maybe giving our will to God means to do things we don't want to sometimes, when God asks us to. Why don't we want to do them? Usually it's because there's some sort of pain associated with it. We tend not to worry about feeling pain when we understand it, but sometimes we don't. So, maybe giving our will to God means to endure pain for Him, even when we don't want to or don't understand. Can we endure pain for God? It's simple on paper but difficult in practice.

When we get to the next life, we probably won't regret having endured hard trial, but we may regret letting it distract us from doing good through Christ. Pain is real, but also it seems like it's the nature of pain to end. Pain holds on as long as it can, but it's just a feeling, so once it's gone it's gone, maybe. It requires some amount of work to persist? Death is lasting, but Christ has overcome death for us. As I look back in my own life, I realize the pain of yesterday has no power to hurt me today, just like yesterday's meal has no power to satisfy today's hunger. There might be pain today related to yesterday's pain, but that's today's pain, and I know that will end eventually as well, through Christ. In everything that God has given us, maybe the biggest things we should be grateful for are the things that last: He gave His Son who can grant the best dream we could hope for; He gives us real work and responsibility, family, employers, relationships, which helps us focus on more than just fleeting pains and desires in this life, and allows us to do something that lasts: helping others.

When Christ was suffering in the garden of Gethsemane, He prayed that, if the Father were willing, to remove the bitter cup. It was not removed, but God answered the prayer by sending an angel to "strengthen" Christ. Sometimes we're called as angels to strengthen others in their trials and help provide comfort, pointing them to Christ. We're not always called to help fix the trial itself, but to ease fear and bring joy, through Christ, even in pain. What is strength? Is it joy, understanding, faith, or love? I'm not sure exactly what the angel did or said. That would be a big responsibility. What can you do or say in a moment like that? Whatever they said, we have the opportunity to do and say those same things as we try to comfort others in Christ in this life.

"There’s surely somewhere a lowly place
In earth’s harvest fields so wide
Where I may labor through life’s short day
For Jesus, the Crucified." - Lyrics from hymn: "I'll Go Where You Want Me To Go".

Even if it feels hard to be happy, we can still live after the manner of happiness through Christ. This means obeying God's commandments, trying to serve others -- even if that looks different than how some others or most other may consider service to look like, appreciating life and relationships -- even if those relationships may not be exactly what we know they will some day have the potential to become, appreciating nature, the world, and God's blessings -- even if we don't currently have the capacity to experience them the way we one day will, making a routine, being responsible, giving gratitude to God and others, being humble, "be[ing] not afraid, only believ[ing]", focusing on Christ and finding our joy in Him. Our life will take a turn for the better when we start to do the mundane or hard tasks of life specifically to show love for God. Eating a vegetable, going to bed on time, seeing a friend, reading a scripture -- doing each of these specifically to show love for Him -- doing them intentionally, because we trust that He knows best and because we know He wants us to do them -- not because we want to do them but because He does, and because we love Him, we want to do them.

"...the word[...] had had more powerful effect upon the mind[...] than the sword..." - Alma's reasoning about the word of God. The word of God can have a powerful effect on our mind and heart too, when we truly need God's power.

If life is going to be hard no matter what we do, what do we really need? Comfort? There's a reason one of God's first titles is "Comforter". The best way to find comfort is to be where Christ is -- in the right place, at the right time, doing the right thing. This seems like a hard task. It's not so much about our ability, but our honesty. It's simple enough for a child to understand and do, in fact, it will require us to become like a child before Christ, trusting Him and wanting to always be near Him.

Healing is what we want, yet Christ promised healing to all those who would have faith in Him. So, maybe what we really need is faith? Faith leads to healing, yet, healing is not a great indicator of faith. A better indicator of faith is faith itself. What is faith? We know it's the opposite of fear. We also know that "perfect love casteth out fear". Is faith love? Faith in Christ is the pure love of Christ -- charity. It is also trust, and agency. Agency is the ability to choose, but, if we choose out of fear, are we really agents -- or are we objects? God never takes our agency, so, He never uses fear. Maybe agency then is choosing God, the one person who allows us to keep it -- not because anything is making us, but because we choose to. We know that we must be possessed of charity -- love, to use our agency correctly. Fear seems to comes naturally, yet charity requires us to ask for it and to seek it, from God. What is faith in Christ then? It's agency -- obeying God, out of love for Him -- even when other voices try to persuade us otherwise with fear. Maybe then, those who follow God have been healed, even as they wait.

"A double-minded man is unstable in all his ways." It's not honest with God to say we put our full trust in Him, and then to adjust that trust based on what we see. That's not trust, but fear. We think we're in control by doing this, but we're actually giving our agency to fear. Control is trust in God. Agency is trust in God. Fear is a loss of control.



Faith in Christ is not simply waiting, though it could be. Faith in Him is doing what He says, and not what fear demands of us. Pain is not a tool of the devil, only fear. Resist fear, but not pain. We were not given agency to avoid pain, but to choose it's type. God's goal is not to have us avoid all pain in this life, but simply to learn something from it, and to accomplish something despite, or because of, it. God promised us blessings for obeying His commandments. He never asked us to measure our obedience by the blessings we've received. If you're obeying a commandment, you're being blessed, whether you know it or not.

As we try to understand this life that God's given us, it's important to understand what we actually agreed to when we came to live on earth. Sometimes we might mistakenly advocate satan's plan instead of God's, even when we know the restored gospel of Christ. God did promise us blessings in this life, but not heaven, until we reach the next. He promised us that He'd give us agency, which means He won't use immediate consequences for our actions to try to sway us into choosing Him over other masters. He didn't promise us a life without pain, even if we honestly try to follow Him, though He did promise us comfort, through our "Comforter": His Son, and the Holy Ghost. He didn't promise us that we'd live in a celestial society, even if we are trying to live that law; We will regularly interact with others who will be living both higher or lower laws than we are trying to. This helps us to see for ourselves why following God is important.

It doesn't matter so much what you're being asked to do, but who's asking you -- whether it's Christ or not. Don't listen to just any voice that promises to spare you pain. That's acting in fear, not faith in Christ. Maybe no one who's honestly doing what they think God wants them to do should need to worry that they're not following the Spirit, because that's the definition of following the Spirit -- doing what you honestly think God wants you to do. God is powerful enough to direct our life, even when we don't understand it.
