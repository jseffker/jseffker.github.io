Sometimes it's easy to think that for those who are righteous, this life should be "a state of rest, a state of peace, where they shall rest from all their troubles and from all care, and sorrow." (Alma 40:12). But, the author of this quote was speaking about those who had left this life when he said that. This life is often the opposite, and is very hard. But didn't Christ say "all things are possible to him that believeth?" (Mark 9:23), and "he that hath faith in me to be healed, and is not appointed unto death, shall be healed." (D&C 42:48)? It's easy to worry that having trials in our lives means our faith in Christ isn't strong enough, and even that God is upset with our lack of faith. Yet, isn't faith just that -- to not worry about when we're healed because we know that we are healed in His time, through Him? Is God displeased that we haven't found relief from our trials, even though we feel Him giving us peace to endure them and serve despite the discomfort? But, let's say, hypothetically, if we lived perfectly, wouldn't we find relief from our trial? Maybe, but it would definitely mean that you probably shouldn't be on earth, because you're a perfect being living among mortals, and don't need God's plan. No, none of us can find out for sure what would happen to our trial if we were perfect, but we can wonder. In the meantime, we can have peace knowing that God never expected us to be perfect. That's why He sent Jesus Christ. So maybe having trials that don't seem to end doesn't mean I've ruined God's plan, and that I'm doing something wrong? Christ said "He who hath faith to see shall see. He who hath faith to hear shall hear. The lame who hath faith to leap shall leap." (D&C 42:49-51). He never said when -- just that they would. And don't forget the other words he said, that "blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed. "
